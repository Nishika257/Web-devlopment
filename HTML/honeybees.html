About Honeybees

They communicate through dance: When a worker bee finds an excellent source of nectar, she returns to the hive and performs the "waggle dance." This precise movement communicates the exact direction and distance of the food to the other bees.

They have five eyes: Honeybees have two large compound eyes on the sides of their head to see shapes and colors, plus three smaller simple eyes (ocelli) on top of their head to detect light levels and navigation cues.

They possess two separate stomachs: One stomach is for digesting their own food. The second stomach acts as a specialized "honey crop" used exclusively for storing and transporting collected nectar back to the hive.

A single bee makes very little honey: In her entire lifetime, an average worker bee will produce only about 1/12th of a teaspoon of honey. It takes a massive team effort from thousands of bees to keep the hive fed.

They can recognize human faces: Research shows that honeybees can remember specific configurations of visual features, allowing them to distinguish between different human faces.
